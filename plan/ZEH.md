## answer

To sharpen the mathematical derivation of backreaction, we can map it directly to Kenneth Wilson’s philosophy of the Renormalization Group (RG).
In standard cosmology, we commit a severe violation of Wilsonian physics: we take a highly non-linear, inhomogeneous metric at small scales (the real universe) and assume we can "smooth" it out into a single homogeneous metric (the FLRW metric) across all scales.
As Wilson noted, we don't have one static theory of everything; we have an effective field theory (EFT) at each scale, and moving between them generates non-trivial corrections. In cosmology, the Buchert Equations serve as the explicit, non-perturbative RG flow equations that track what happens when you coarse-grain the universe.
Here is the precise derivation of how this "scale-shifting" generates an apparent Dark Energy.

1. The Scaling Problem (The Covariant Average)

Let \(V\) be a compact spatial domain (a macroscopic scale). We define the spatial average of any scalar quantity \(f(t, \mathbf{x})\) over this volume as:
\(\langle f\rangle _{V}\equiv \frac{1}{V}\int _{V}f\sqrt{det(q)}\,d^{3}x\)
where \(q_{ij}\) is the physical, inhomogeneous spatial metric.
Because the metric itself determines the volume element, averaging and time-derivation do not commute. This is the fundamental source of backreaction, analogous to the generation of operator corrections under an RG coarse-graining step:
\(\partial _{t}\langle f\rangle _{V}-\langle \partial _{t}f\rangle _{V}=\langle f\theta \rangle _{V}-\langle f\rangle _{V}\langle \theta \rangle _{V}\)
where \(\theta = \nabla_i v^i\) is the local expansion rate of the cosmic fluid.

2. The Effective Scale Equations (The Cosmological RG Flow)

If we average the exact local Einstein Field Equations (for irrotational dust) over the domain \(V\), we get the Buchert Equations. These are the effective equations of motion at scale \(V\):
Effective Friedmann Acceleration Equation:
\(3\frac{\"{a}_{V}}{a_{V}}=-4\pi G\langle \rho \rangle _{V}+\mathcal{Q}_{V}\)
Effective Fluid Equation:
\(3\left(\frac{\.{a}_{V}}{a_{V}}\right)^{2}=8\pi G\langle \rho \rangle _{V}-\frac{1}{2}\langle \mathcal{R}\rangle _{V}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{Q}_{V}\)
where \(a_V(t) \equiv (V(t)/V_0)^{1/3}\) is the scale factor specific to the volume scale \(V\), \(\langle \mathcal{R} \rangle_V\) is the average spatial curvature, and \(\mathcal{Q}_{V}\) is the Kinematical Backreaction Term.

3. The "Wilsonian" Boundary Term: \(\mathcal{Q}_{V}\)

The term \(\mathcal{Q}_{V}\) acts exactly like the high-energy loop corrections or "running constants" generated when integrating out short-distance degrees of freedom:
\(\mathcal{Q}_{V}\equiv \frac{2}{3}\left(\langle \theta ^{2}\rangle _{V}-\langle \theta \rangle _{V}^{2}\right)-2\langle \sigma ^{2}\rangle _{V}\)
• \(\langle \theta^2 \rangle_V - \langle \theta \rangle_V^2\) (The Variance): This measures the statistical variance in expansion rates between different sub-regions (e.g., fast voids vs. slow walls). It is strictly positive.
• \(\langle \sigma^2 \rangle_V\) (The Shear): This measures the anisotropic stretching and tearing of matter as it collapses into filaments. It is strictly positive.

4. Splitting the Scales: Generating the Illusion of \(\Lambda \)

To see how Räsänen and Wiltshire use this to eliminate Dark Energy, we apply a Wilsonian multi-scale split. Let's partition the macro-volume \(V\) into two distinct scale-domains: Voids (\(v\)) and Walls (\(w\)), with volume fractions \(f_v(t) + f_w(t) = 1\).
The global scale factor \(a_{V}\) relates to the sub-scale factors via:
\(a_{V}^{3}=f_{v0}a_{v}^{3}+f_{w0}a_{w}^{3}\)
If we compute the global expansion rate \(H_V \equiv \dot{a}_V/a_V\), it becomes a volume-weighted average of the local scales plus a dynamical boundary term:
\(H_{V}=f_{v}H_{v}+f_{w}H_{w}\)
If we differentiate this with respect to time to find the acceleration, the non-commuting nature of the scales manifests explicitly:
\(\frac{\"{a}_{V}}{a_{V}}=f_{v}\frac{\"{a}_{v}}{a_{v}}+f_{w}\frac{\"{a}_{w}}{a_{w}}+\mathcal{J}_{\text{scale}}\)
where the "inter-scale" current is:
\(\mathcal{J}_{\text{scale}}=2f_{v}f_{w}(H_{v}-H_{w})^{2}+\.{f}_{v}(H_{v}-H_{w})\)

5. The Renormalized "Dark Energy"

In a smooth FLRW universe, \(\mathcal{Q}_V = 0\) and \(\mathcal{J}_{\text{scale}} = 0\). Because gravity is attractive, \(\"{a}_{V}\) must be negative (deceleration).
However, in the real universe:
1. Voids expand much faster than walls (\(H_v \gg H_w\)).
2. Voids occupy more volume over time (\(\dot{f}_v > 0\)).
Therefore, the variance term \(\mathcal{Q}_{V}\) becomes highly positive, and the scale-current \(\mathcal{J}_{\text{scale}}\) acts as a net positive driver. If we force-fit this multi-scale reality into a single-scale FLRW template—which is what cosmologists do when interpreting Supernovae data—we are forced to invent a fictitious constant to balance the ledger:
\(\Lambda _{\text{apparent}}\equiv \mathcal{Q}_{V}-\frac{1}{3}\int \frac{\.{\mathcal{Q}}_{V}}{a_{V}^{3}}dt\)
The Räsänen-Wiltshire proposal can be summarized in Wilson's language: Dark energy is not a fundamental field (\(\Lambda = 0\) at the micro-scale); it is a phantom operator generated strictly by the Renormalization Group flow (\(\mathcal{Q}_V > 0\)) when averaging up to the horizon scale.
